# Fisheye rig calibration: research and validation plan

The goal is one metric, rigid calibration for the forward wide, forward narrow, and rear-facing cabin cameras. Spherical rendering is a separate validation step. Do not promote a candidate or start batch rendering until it passes held-out tests.

## Relevant methods and sources

- [CamOdoCal](https://github.com/hengli/camodocal): vehicle odometry and visual pose estimation give a practical calibration route for cameras that do not share scene points. The existing one-pair essential-matrix test throws away too much information. Track static features over multi-second clips and bundle-adjust their observations; use odometry and IMU as constraints, not exact truth.
- [Kalibr camera–IMU calibration](https://github.com/ethz-asl/kalibr/wiki/Camera-IMU-calibration) and [rolling-shutter calibration](https://github.com/ethz-asl/kalibr/wiki/Rolling-Shutter-Camera-Calibration): continuous-time trajectories make it possible to evaluate each observation at its actual capture time and estimate temporal offsets. Kalibr's standard [multi-camera target workflow](https://github.com/ethz-asl/kalibr/wiki/Multiple-camera-calibration) expects overlapping neighboring views; do not assume it can directly join disconnected camera pairs without a common IMU, known targets, or another shared reference.
- [The Double Sphere Camera Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08957) and [Kalibr supported models](https://github.com/ethz-asl/kalibr/wiki/Supported-models): compare an equidistant model with radial corrections, Kannala–Brandt/equidistant, and Double Sphere on held-out rays and straight structures near the image edges. A model that only wins in the narrow overlap is not validated for the cabin side windows.
- [GTSAM](https://github.com/borglab/gtsam) is one implementation option for tightly coupled visual–inertial factor graphs. [Spherical Video V2 RFC](https://github.com/google/spatial-media/blob/master/docs/spherical-video-v2-rfc.md) specifies projection metadata; tagging does not repair bad calibration, missing coverage, or parallax.

## Audit results on this rig

`rig_audit.py` successfully read 74 sampled segments, with 3,237 one-second gyro bins. The logged camera sensor identifier was OS04C10. Gyro sampling was about 101.4 Hz, and gyro and camera times overlapped in all 74 segments. By matching camera state frame IDs, the median cabin exposure-midpoint phase was +24.98 ms relative to wide; narrow was effectively simultaneous (−0.013 ms median). Use capture timestamps, not frame-ID equality, in a motion optimizer. Rotation excitation singular values were 1 : 0.086 : 0.059; most rotation was about one axis. There are useful turns, but the weaker axes need explicit observability checks before solving readout, intrinsics, and full extrinsics together. The local machine-readable report is `previews/rig-audit.json`.

## Dataset-specific approach

1. Run `uv run rig_audit.py` to verify sensor identities, timestamp domains, frame-phase offsets, and rotational excitation. Use the same timestamp convention end to end. Frame start/end timestamps establish a candidate exposure interval, not the rolling-shutter row model. Confirm sensor axis mapping and readout against device source or measurement.
2. Build a small diverse corpus: straight highway for travel direction, grade transitions and turns for observability, stationary periods for gravity and gyro bias, and clean barriers, vertical posts, and distant skyline for validation. Mask the cabin, glass reflections, vehicles, and dashboard. Keep entire routes out of training. Straight highway alone does not identify full 6-DoF extrinsics, distortion, and timing.
3. Fit intrinsics first. Tie wide/cabin intrinsics as requested, but verify both at the edges individually. Prefer a fixed-mount calibration session with a large AprilGrid at many tilts, positions, and distances. For nonoverlapping views, use surveyed targets in a common frame, or calibrate each camera to the same IMU while moving the rigid rig through sufficiently varied motion. Do not change the mounts between recording and calibration. If no new capture is available, report weakly observable parameters rather than forcing a solution.
4. Use the wide camera or IMU as the fixed reference frame. Track static features independently per camera across multiple frames. Gradually enable parameters: trajectory and per-clip biases, camera extrinsics, time offsets/readout if observable, then shared intrinsic refinements. Use robust image reprojection losses, gyro/accel factors, and appropriate priors; use gravity to level the sphere, and road lines/odometry to set heading. Estimate translation if supported, but do not expect a rotation-only stitch to align nearby objects at all depths.
5. Validate separately: forward overlap with held-out static feature angular error and edge coverage; rear alignment with multi-frame barrier/road directions and gravity-consistent horizon at both windows; timestamp and dynamic seam review at 4K in the headset. Render clips before rendering drives. Near objects can require a depth-aware warp or seam selection; no calibration fills areas the sensor never saw.

The existing `calibration-shared.json` is a diagnostic candidate, not a validated rig calibration. Its focal length hit a fitting bound. The barrier at `2026-11-15T21:47:49.988Z` is a useful targeted check but not sufficient evidence by itself.
